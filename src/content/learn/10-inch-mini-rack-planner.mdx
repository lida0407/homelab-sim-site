## The short answer

10-inch racks have no formal standard, but they share a common set of dimensions:

| Dimension | 10-inch | 19-inch (for comparison) |
|---|---|---|
| Front panel width | 254 mm | 482.6 mm |
| Hole spacing (centre to centre) | ≈ 235 mm | 465.1 mm |
| Clear width between rails | ≈ 222 mm | ≈ 450 mm |
| 1 rack unit (U) | 44.45 mm | 44.45 mm |
| Depth | varies by maker | varies by maker |

Measure your own rack's opening and depth before buying tight-fitting gear. Some 10-inch racks are a millimetre or two narrower.

Example: a 200 × 190 × 50 mm mini PC in a 250 mm deep 10" rack fits, with 22 mm of width and 10 mm of depth to spare, and takes 2U on a shelf.

## What usually goes in a 10-inch rack

| Item | Typical U | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Patch panel (6–12 ports) | 1U | Keystone panels let you mix Cat6, fibre and HDMI |
| Switch (5–16 ports) | 1U | Many small switches fit on a 1U shelf or with 10" ears |
| Mini PCs (1-litre class) | 1–2U per shelf | Usually on a vented shelf; mind the power bricks |
| Single-board computers | 1U for 2–4 boards | Use a mount with airflow |
| 2–4 bay NAS | 3–5U on a shelf | Check depth first; 3.5" drive NAS units run deep |
| PDU or USB-C power | 1U | Count the bricks: they need space too |
| Small UPS | 2–4U on a shelf, or under the rack | Heavy: keep it at the bottom |
| Blanks / vented panels | 1U | Close the gaps so air goes through the gear |

## Laying it out

1. **Network at the top, power at the bottom.** Patch panel and switch in the top units keep patch cables short; the UPS and the heaviest gear go at the bottom.
2. **Compute in the middle, with air.** Mini PCs pull air front to back or side to side. Leave a vented panel or a gap between hot shelves, and don't close a cabinet door on 150 W without a fan.
3. **Plan the power bricks.** In a 10-inch rack the bricks, not the PCs, often decide the layout. A 10-inch PDU, a strip on the back rail or a shared USB-C PD supply saves space.
4. **Leave 20–30 % free.** You will add something.
5. **Know your watts.** A minilab of a switch, three mini PCs and a NAS often idles under 100 W, but measure: idle draw is what you pay for all month.
